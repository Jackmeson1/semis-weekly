## INV-01 AI / token demand will not peak · L0 · Stated

- 2024-06: the author argued that a bull market depends on return on investment, and is essentially a contest between the technology revolution and long-term interest rates.
- 2025-11: the author judged that until scaling laws are clearly disproven, capex has no reason to slow down.
- 2026-07, rewritten: a model company's ARR may fall, but the demand is for tokens and does not depend on any single company.
- 2026-10, rewritten again: what matters now is whether the model companies (labs) can make money and whether they have the cash.
- **Threat signals**: C5's S-L5-03 (hyperscaler capex year over year) falls below watch; S-L5-01 and S-L5-02 weaken for consecutive periods; C1's S-L3-03 reaches binding (demand is still there, but there is no money to pay for it).
- **Not a threat**: bubble talk, a single quarterly earnings miss, product delays (NOVETO-03 and NOVETO-04 in aggregation.md).
- **From the videos · the main line is whether model companies can make money** (from 2026-06-02): as long as the revenue growth of the two model companies can support cloud capex, rates, oil, inflation and product delays are not threats. Once this line moves, the whole market would collapse. The author checks Anthropic's annualized revenue growth in the middle of each month, and a lower "acceleration" is dangerous enough. The author treats it as a real threat only when both "revenue increments" and "model quality or commoditization" are questioned together. Changes in how revenue is counted, and slowdowns caused by a shortage of compute, do not count. The data is only media reports, so write it in the log only.
- **From the videos · the arithmetic of sustainability**: if 1 trillion is invested in a year, the model layer has to earn 200 to 300 billion (about 20% to 30% of capex).
- **From the videos · the time horizon of certainty has changed**: in 2025-12 the author said "next year is certain, the year after is not"; from 2026-05 the author said shortages last to 2028, and that several upstream companies say the shortage cycle is 3 to 5 years. Give the date when citing.
- **From the videos · bubble talk is still ignored** (14 videos, consistent over two years): most of the rise came from earnings rather than valuation expansion, and the buyers are profitable giants inside the system, unlike 2000. The author's watch points for a short-term top: OpenAI going consumer-facing or listing with expectations fully priced, and the AI application layer being bid up sky-high.

## INV-02 US Treasury supply weighs on the long end; a rate cut does not mean lower long-end yields · L2/L1 · Stated

- 2024-05: the author saw sustained heavy issuance as the biggest underappreciated risk to US dollar rates.
- 2025-01: the author cautioned against assuming that liquidity will spill over as soon as rates are cut, and said their view on long-term yields had not changed.
- 2026-09, rewritten: the level of rates is not the problem and is not why markets fall; what matters is volatility.
- **How to use it**: a Fed cut alone does not imply "liquidity improves". Look at C2's speed and at auctions.
- **From the videos · this one has seasons**: from 2024-05 to 2025-01 the author treated supply as the biggest risk to the long end; in mid-2025, with supply down year over year and banks able to buy more Treasuries, it was not the main line; from 2026-09 it became a pressure again. What weighs on the long end is net supply counted by duration (see C2).
- **From the videos · debt growth supports nominal growth**: government debt is growing about 10% and private debt about 1% to 3%, so while fiscal policy keeps expanding the odds of a hard landing are low. In 2026-06 the author turned to the long-term turning point where "rates are above growth" (the buffer is about -2.0 percentage points and has not flipped yet).

## INV-03 Semiconductors are a supply-planned economy; shortages follow capacity · L5 · Stated

- 2026-01: the author described semiconductors as a purely supply-driven industry: once the big manufacturers decide to add capacity, the broad picture of strong demand and shortages will not change, so there is no need to worry.
- The author's 2024 stock-picking method: infer NVDA shipments and EPS from TSMC's CoWoS capacity allocation, and sell once the information gap closes.
- **How to use it**: when picking stocks, look at who has capacity and who sits on the bottleneck. TSMC's monthly revenue in C5 is the first-hand reading.
- **From the videos · shortages happen where the sellers control capacity**: TSMC controls capacity, so GPUs and CPUs stay short; memory and optical modules can expand and may become commodities in two or three years. In a shortage, pick chips by performance and unit count, not by performance per watt.

## INV-04 Funding is the ultimate constraint · L3 · Stated (appeared only in late 2025)

- 2025-11: at the time the author did not yet weigh it, saying the topic could wait until data-center asset-backed bonds were being sold to pension funds.
- 2026-07, raised to the top: a falling stock price can be endured, but deteriorating credit cannot. As long as credit does not break down sharply, one can stay firmly bullish; balance-sheet runoff or war does not change that.
- 2026-07, admitted an error: one cannot ignore the macro funding backdrop just because the company and industry layers look strong.
- **How to use it**: C1 is the first step in the order of checks.
- **From the videos · early signs**: in 2024-07 the author already ranked funding ahead of returns (if the rate curve breaks first there is no money to invest), but in 2024-08 said companies using AI cloud do not depend on funding, and in 2025-09 saw circular financing as a resource in the compute war rather than funding pressure. So the 2025-11 upgrade was an accurate synthesis, but it did not start from zero.
- **From the videos · a sign of the late stage of a bubble**: data-center debt moves into off-balance-sheet securitization, with hedge funds buying the junior tranches and pension funds buying the senior ones.
